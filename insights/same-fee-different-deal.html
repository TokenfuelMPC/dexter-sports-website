Same fee, different deal: why scope matters more than the number

Two offers. Same payment. Very different commitments.

When an offer arrives, the number gets all the attention. But payment alone can't describe what an athlete is agreeing to. Here's a hypothetical comparison from our Before You Sign toolkit.

Proposed termOffer AOffer B
Cash compensation$750$750
Athlete's workOne videoOne video
Brand reuse30 days on brand social accounts12 months of paid advertising
Category restrictionNone statedSix months
Athlete-paid costs$100$100
Representative fee$75$75
Cash before taxes$575$575

What the arithmetic tells you

Both leave the same estimated cash after listed costs: $750 − $100 − $75 = $575 before taxes.

What still needs discussion

Offer B asks for a much longer advertising period, and paid advertising at that, plus a six-month restriction on competing partnerships. That could mean turning down a better opportunity in the same category next spring.

None of that makes Offer B wrong. It means the family should ask whether the full scope fits the athlete's goals, and whether to discuss changes to the rights, the compensation, or the restriction.

Compare scope as well as money. The example doesn't determine which offer to accept. It shows why the payment alone can't describe the full commitment.
